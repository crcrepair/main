## What phone battery health actually measures

Every phone runs on a lithium-ion battery, and every lithium-ion battery wears out chemically as it is charged and discharged. Battery health, which Apple calls maximum capacity, is the charge the battery can hold today as a percentage of what it held when new. At 85%, a full charge gets you roughly 85% of the running time the phone once had.

Wear is counted in charge cycles. One cycle is 100% of the battery's capacity used, whether in a single day or spread over two days at 50% each. Apple says the iPhone 14 and earlier are designed to keep 80% of their capacity after 500 full cycles, and the iPhone 15 and later after 1,000 cycles, in ideal conditions. In practice, most phones start to feel the loss after two to three years of daily use.

## How to check iPhone battery health

1. Open **Settings**.
2. Tap **Battery**.
3. Tap **Battery Health** (labelled Battery Health & Charging on older versions of iOS).
4. Read the **Maximum Capacity** figure.

On the iPhone 15 and later, the same screen shows the cycle count, along with when the battery was made and first used. If iOS judges the battery to be significantly worn, this screen will also tell you it needs servicing.

The percentage is an estimate, and it can lag behind how the phone behaves. A battery that reads 84% but shuts down at 30% on a cold platform is in worse shape than its number suggests, and that is where a proper test helps. ## How to check battery health on Samsung

Samsung keeps its battery test in the Samsung Members app, which comes pre-installed on Galaxy phones.

1. Open **Samsung Members**.
2. Tap **Support**.
3. Tap **Phone diagnostics**, then **Battery status**.

The result rates the battery's life as Good, Normal or Weak. You can reach the same test from Settings, under Device care and then Diagnostics. Some newer Galaxy models also list battery information, including how many times the battery has been charged, under Settings and then Battery.

## What your battery health percentage means

| Battery health | What it means | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 90–100% | As good as new. | Nothing. If the phone still drains quickly, look at apps and settings first. |
| 80–89% | Normal wear. The phone gets through a shorter day than it used to. | Keep an eye on it. Think about a new battery if you are charging by mid-afternoon. |
| Below 80% | Worn. The lost capacity shows up in everyday use. | Replace the battery. |
| Any figure, with swelling | The battery is failing physically. | Stop using and charging the phone and have it looked at straight away. |

Below 80%, an iPhone may also slow itself down to avoid switching off unexpectedly, so a worn battery can make the whole phone feel old.

## Signs your battery needs attention, whatever the number says

- **Phone battery draining fast.** Same apps and same routine, but the charge that used to reach the evening is gone by mid-afternoon.
- **Sudden drops or shutdowns.** The percentage falls from 30% to single figures in minutes, or the phone switches off while still showing charge, especially on a cold morning.
- **Heat with light use.** The handset warms up while you are only reading email.
- **It only works plugged in.** The phone dies the moment the cable comes out.

Before you blame the battery, open Settings and then Battery to see which apps have used the most power. One runaway app can empty a healthy battery, and a phone that has just had a big software update can drain quickly for a day or two. If the usage list looks normal and the phone still will not last, the battery is the likely cause. ## A swollen phone battery is not one to wait on

If the screen is lifting at one edge, the back panel is bulging or the phone rocks on a flat table, the battery has probably swollen. Lithium batteries give off gas as they fail, and a swollen one can catch fire if it is punctured or kept on charge.

- Stop charging it and switch it off.
- Do not press the screen back down or try to open the phone.
- Keep it away from anything flammable. Do not post it or put it in the household rubbish.
- Take it to a repair shop as soon as you can.

## How to keep your battery health higher for longer

- **Keep it cool.** Heat ages a battery faster than anything else. Do not leave the phone on a radiator shelf, a sunny windowsill or a car dashboard.
- **Use the charge limit.** The iPhone 15 and later, and recent Samsung and Pixel phones, can stop charging at around 80%. If you sit near a charger all day, switch it on.
- **Use a decent charger and cable.** Cheap ones deliver uneven power and run hot. We stock [genuine Apple and Samsung chargers](https://www.crcrepair.co.uk/accessories/) in store.
- **Do not run it flat as a habit.** Topping up little and often is kinder than going from 100% to zero.

## Battery health check FAQs

### How do I check my phone's battery health?

On an iPhone, go to Settings > Battery > Battery Health. On a Samsung, open Samsung Members and go to Support > Phone diagnostics > Battery status. On a Pixel 8a or later, go to Settings > Battery > Battery health. If your phone does not give a reading, a repair shop can test it for you.

### At what battery health should I replace my iPhone battery?

Below 80% maximum capacity. Replace it sooner if the phone shuts down unexpectedly or no longer lasts the day.

### Why does my phone die faster in cold weather?

Cold temporarily reduces the power a lithium-ion battery can deliver. A healthy battery copes, but a worn one can drop sharply or switch off. If it happens regularly, the battery is probably due for replacement.
